# Gargantua Bench

Generated by `bench/report.py` from measured artifacts. Reproduce:

```bash
pipeline/.venv/Scripts/python bench/run_bench.py        # synthetic clips: metrics + full pipeline builds
pipeline/.venv/Scripts/python bench/build_corpus.py     # real clips (docs/CLIP_LICENSES.md)
node tools/bench/perf.mjs --out <name> <volumes...>     # fps / VRAM at 1920x1080
node tools/bench/replica_render.mjs && pipeline/.venv/Scripts/python tools/bench/replica_score.py
node tools/bench/calibrate.mjs                          # look constants fitted to the reel
pipeline/.venv/Scripts/python bench/report.py
```

Reading: the temporal **mode** recovers the background exactly while it is the most frequent colour, which holds down to about 20% visibility on random-coloured crowds; below ~10% visibility every estimator fails and the mode is worse than the median (a slow passer-by out-dwells the background). The **hybrid** (median / mode / run-capped mode, scored by support x separate visits, median fallback below 12% support) is no worse than the median on any synthetic clip and better on the dense ones. A crowd in uniformly dark clothes (`dense_crowd_dark`) is the adversarial case: the dark clothing *is* the most frequent colour.

**Verdict.** Smallest tier: lowest moving rate 714 fps (target 60: met). Largest tiers (your clips): lowest moving rate 72 fps, lowest idle refinement pass 34 fps (target 30: met). Across all 16 real clips the moving rate is at least 56 fps. One volume refines below 30 fps when idle: wp25_times_square_billboard, 26 fps.
Rebuilding the heaviest one at `--tier M` (628x540x450) gives 70 fps idle and 112 fps moving.

Frame times are synchronised with a 1-pixel `readPixels` (Chrome's `gl.finish()` does not block) and cross-checked against `EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2`. Moving = half the ray steps at 70% resolution (what you see while orbiting); idle = full steps, full resolution, then progressive accumulation stops re-rendering once converged.
